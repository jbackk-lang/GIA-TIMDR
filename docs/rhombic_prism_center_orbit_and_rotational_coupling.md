# Central-point geometry, limiting orbits, and rotational coupling

Research note (2026-11-13). This document distinguishes exact mathematical deductions from computational questions and physical hypotheses. It is not a claim of a new physical force.

## Model

The triangular base has vertices A=(0,0,0), B=(1,0,0), C=(1/2,sqrt(3)/2,0). The upper base is translated by t=(u,v,w), where w=sqrt(1-u²-v²)>0. The centroid of the homogeneous prism is

O=( (1+u)/2, sqrt(3)/6+v/2, w/2 ).

This is the midpoint of the two triangular-base centroids. In general, the triangular prism is **not** centrally symmetric.

The five generators are reflections in the bottom and top planes and in the three affine side-face planes. For an affine isometry g(x)=Rx+b, the orbit of the central point is g(O)=RO+b.

## Exact results

**Unbounded group and center orbit.** Reflections in z=0 and z=w compose to the translation T(x)=x+2w e_z. Thus T^n(O)=O+2nw e_z for every integer n, and the full orbit of O is unbounded. No compact global limit in ordinary Euclidean space can contain this entire orbit.

**Unbounded family of rotation axes.** Let R be the composition of reflection in z=0 with reflection in any lateral plane. Its axis is the intersection of the planes, a horizontal base-edge line with unit direction a perpendicular to e_z. The conjugates T^n R T^{-n} rotate around translated axes, whose Pluecker coordinates are (a,m_0+2nw(e_z×a)). Consequently their moment norms diverge as |n|→∞. This does not imply that every sequence of axes diverges.

**A limiting direction for that family.** For n→+∞, m_n/||m_n||→e_z×a; for n→-∞ the sign reverses. These are explicit asymptotic directions for a subfamily, not a formula for the entire limiting set.

**Pluecker conditions.** For a line with unit direction a and closest-point vector p perpendicular to a, m=p×a satisfies ||a||=1 and a·m=0. Unoriented lines identify (a,m) with (-a,-m). A sign-invariant distance is min(||L1-L2||,||L1+L2||), though this is a coordinate metric and depends on the chosen length units.

**Action of affine isometries on lines.** For g(x)=Rx+b, with R orthogonal, the image of (a,m) is (Ra, det(R) Rm+b×Ra). This follows from (Rx)×(Ra)=det(R)R(x×a).

**Central-point screw decomposition.** For a proper rigid displacement with rotation angle θ, screw axis unit direction a, axis point p, and axial translation h, put ρ=||(O-p)×a||. Then

||g(O)-O||² = 4ρ² sin²(θ/2)+h².

The quantity W=4ρ² sin²(θ/2) is the squared transverse displacement of the center for that displacement; it is not in general invariant under group composition.

## Toward a limiting-object equation

For the finite sets S_n={g(O): g is represented by a word of length ≤n}, the nested union S=∪S_n is the center orbit. A mathematically well-defined closed orbit is closure(S). To characterize asymptotic directions, one may study limit points of (g(O)-O)/||g(O)-O|| along sequences whose displacement tends to infinity. This separates the unbounded spatial orbit from its compact directional boundary.

One can analogously compactify line moments by mapping m to m/sqrt(1+||m||²), taking account of the sign identification. A zero-set description via a distance-to-tail function is a *definition* of an accumulation set, not yet an independently derived analytic equation analogous to a spectral zeta function. An analytic transfer operator or functional equation requires a separately justified construction.

## Physical interpretation: hypothesis only

The orbit of the center under discrete isometries is kinematic geometry, not a time evolution. If a continuous rotation about a fixed axis with angular speed ω is imposed, the center's perpendicular acceleration has magnitude ω²ρ and points toward the axis. The outward centrifugal term appears only in the rotating frame. For mass M, the corresponding centripetal force magnitude is Mω²ρ. These standard mechanical relations do not establish an additional force or physical vortex generated by the reflection group.
